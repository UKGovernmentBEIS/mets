The UK CBAM will address greenhouse gas emissions embedded in goods of specific sectors imported into the UK, establishing a carbon price on selected products produced outside the UK ETS so that their importers face a similar incentive to reduce GHG emissions as those under the UK ETS. This will mitigate the risk of carbon leakage in the relevant sectors and needs to be accompanied by a reduction in free allocation under the UK ETS in those sectors.

The CBAM will be phased in gradually and will initially apply to imports of goods from specific sectors defined in Reference . These are summarised in Reference . The goods included are identified using the Combined Nomenclature (CN) codes.

For product benchmark sub-installations CBAM goods are defined based on the CN codes of the products, and this reference should be used to identify if a specific product falls under the CBAM. The baseline data reporting template will take the CBAM factor automatically into account where all goods from a product benchmark are covered by the CBAM. There may be cases where CBAM and non-CBAM goods will both have the same product benchmark. This can be the case where the trade classification (the CN code) does not fully correspond to technical production processes, which are sometimes better reflected by the PRODCOM codes, however this is unlikely.

For fall-back sub-installations, when the heat benchmark, fuel benchmark, and/or the process emissions approach are involved, the application of the CBAM factor will depend on whether the heat, fuel or process emissions are associated with a process manufacturing CBAM goods. Heat exported to a non-ETS installation, where it is used to produce a CBAM good, will be included in a CBAM sub-installation. Therefore, the respective CL sub-installations for these 3 approaches may require an additional split to be made, to provide separate allocation calculations for CBAM goods and non-CBAM goods. This will lead to additional sub-installations, resulting in up to 3 heat sub-installations (CL_CBAM, CL, non-CBAM and non-CL), and similarly up to 3 fuel sub-installations and up to 3 process emissions sub-installations. The district heating sub-installation will not be impacted by the CBAM and will remain unchanged.

For all cases, where the UK CBAM results in the splitting of any sub-installations, this must be made in the baseline data report (BDR) template as appropriate. In such cases the BDR must be revised accordingly to split the relevant data into the sub-installations, re-verified, and uploaded to this workflow. An updated MMP will also be required in order to set out the methodology applied to determine these splits.

Where goods produced within a sub-installation are all CBAM or all non-CBAM goods then sub-installation splitting is not required, and no revisions to the BDR, re-verification or updated MMP is required at this time.
